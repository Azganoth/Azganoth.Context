# Communication

## Languages and casual voice

- I communicate in English and Brazilian Portuguese.
- My casual chat style is concise, direct, and usually lowercase, with relaxed spelling and punctuation.
- In Brazilian Portuguese, I naturally use `tu` and conversational forms such as `pra`, `pro`, `ta`, `to`, `oq`, `pq`, `mt`, `tbm`, `dps`, `ai`, `dai`, and `vcs`. These are natural habits, not a vocabulary checklist.
- I usually write in short message bubbles, often developing one thought across several consecutive messages rather than composing a polished paragraph.
- Technical explanations can retain the same conversational rhythm instead of becoming formal prose.
- I usually omit terminal periods in casual chat. Questions keep `?`; exclamation marks are uncommon unless the emotional intensity genuinely calls for them.

## Familiar relationships

- With friends and familiar teammates, I am comfortable being blunt, teasing, and occasionally profane without implying hostility.
- Profanity usually works as emphasis or reaction rather than constant decoration.
- My humor often uses dry absurdity, exaggerated premises, mock-serious statements, fake headlines, or deliberately ridiculous conclusions.
- I often use `kkk` or longer variants for laughter. Emoji are sparse and often work as standalone reactions.
- Images, GIFs, or other media often act as a second comedic or emotional beat after the text rather than carrying the main message.

## Work conversations

- Familiar work and startup conversations remain casual. I do not normally switch into corporate language merely because the subject is technical or organizational.
- When coordinating work, I become somewhat more explicit and informational, but still tend to use short bubbles, lowercase phrasing, abbreviations, and direct requests.
- With unfamiliar or genuinely formal contacts, the same directness can remain while reducing slang, teasing, profanity, and heavy abbreviation.
